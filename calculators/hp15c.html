How this calculator works

HP-15C: RPN, the numbers first, then the operation

There is no = key. Key the first number, press ENTER, key the second number, then press the operation (HP pp. 22-23): shows

The stack

Numbers wait in a stack of four registers, X, Y, Z and T, and the display shows X (HP p. 32). ENTER copies X into Y, ready for the next number (HP pp. 32-36). A result already in the display needs no ENTER before the next operation (HP p. 22).

Functions of one number

Key the number, then the function (HP p. 22): shows in Degrees mode.

f and g

f, then a key, gives the gold function printed above that key. g gives the blue function printed on the lower face of the key (HP p. 18).

Fractions

There is no a b/c key, so a fraction is keyed as a division: shows

Angle modes

g DEG, g RAD and g GRD set the angle mode (HP p. 26). Degrees mode shows no annunciator. RAD or GRAD shows on the display when that mode is on (HP pp. 31, 60).

Rectangular and polar coordinates

  • Rectangular to polar: y, ENTER, x, then g →P shows r, and x≶y shows θ (HP p. 30). shows , then shows . These are the handbook's own example, 11.1803 and 26.5651 (HP p. 31).
  • Polar to rectangular: θ, ENTER, r, then f →R shows x, and x≶y shows y (HP p. 31). shows

Storage registers

STO 1 stores X in register 1 and RCL 1 brings it back (HP p. 42).

Display

At power-up the display shows four decimal places, FIX 4 (HP p. 58). Every number is held to 10 digits, and every result is rounded to the 10th digit (HP p. 60). A comma separates groups of three digits, as in (HP p. 61).

Clearing

← deletes the last digit while a number is being keyed. g CLx clears the display to zero (HP p. 21).

Watch the keys

Each example is written once and shown in this calculator’s own keys. Run on calculator presses the keys one at a time, and the key being pressed lights up. Speed, Jog and Hide operations on the calculator change how a run plays.
